What to ask

Short, specific questions are easier to say out loud and harder to wave off than big ones. Ask about a line on a page, not about their judgment.

If you get no real answer

"It depends on a lot of factors" is a common reply, and sometimes it is honest. But it is still an answer, and you are allowed to write it down with the date. A pattern of non-answers is information about how your case is being handled, and you gather it without arguing with anyone.

These are questions about your own bill and your own file. Asking them is ordinary. For questions about the law or your legal strategy, speak with a licensed attorney or your court's self-help center.
